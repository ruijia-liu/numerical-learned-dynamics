# Numerical Methods and Learned Dynamics

A computational study of the damped and driven harmonic oscillator, followed by an exploratory neural-network model of its one-step dynamics.

Developed from PHYS20762 Computational Physics coursework by Ruijia Liu. All data are generated by the notebook; no external dataset, API or GPU is needed.

## Explore

Open [the executed notebook](oscillator_dynamics.ipynb) for equations, implementations, plots and discussion.

- Implement Euler, Improved Euler (Heun), Euler-Cromer and an approximate position-Verlet scheme.
- Compare position errors with analytical solutions across damping regimes and time steps.
- Examine pulse forcing, sinusoidal response, resonance, phase lag and force-amplitude dependence.
- Train a two-layer MLP on one simulated trajectory, then compare recursive rollout with the numerical reference.

The system is

$$m\ddot{x}+b\dot{x}+kx=F(t), \qquad m=4.62\ \mathrm{kg},\quad k=3.34\ \mathrm{N/m}.$$

## Interpretation

For the stated damped fixed-step example, Improved Euler has the smallest position RMSE among the four implementations. Its damped error sweep is approximately second order. The retained Verlet scheme is an approximation for velocity-dependent damping and shows lower observed order in that case.

The neural-network section uses a random 80/20 split of points from **one** sinusoidally driven trajectory. A strong one-step score in this setting does not establish independent-trajectory generalisation. Recursive rollout exposes error accumulation and departure from the training states. The model is an exploratory example, not a validated replacement for a numerical solver.

## Limits

- The convergence plots measure position error over a finite step range. Euler-Cromer's near-second-order undamped position result uses a special initial condition and is not a general order claim.
- The Verlet implementation uses a lagged velocity estimate in its damping term; its behavior should not be generalized to other Verlet formulations.
- Resonance and phase estimates use finite grids and time windows. A damped amplitude peak lies below the natural frequency; the theoretical phase lag is 90 degrees at the natural frequency.
- The driven reference settles into periodic motion, not zero displacement. A learned rollout approaching zero would therefore fail to reproduce the forcing.
- No speedup, cross-parameter accuracy or general-purpose learned integrator is claimed.
